TOPIC
-----
US Retail Sales Drop

KEY_FACTS
---------
- US retail sales fell 0.6% last month, marking the first drop in nine months and the largest since May 2025.
- Sales at nonstore online retailers dropped 2.2% in July following a 7.7% spike in June.
- Consumer sentiment slid about 8% this month according to University of Michigan survey results.
- The US unexpectedly lost 23,000 jobs last month while annual inflation hovered around 3.4%.

MAIN_INSIGHT
------------
The July retail sales dip isn't just a post-Prime Day hangover; it's a clear signal that high inflation and cooling job growth are finally catching up to consumer wallets.

LINKEDIN_POST
-------------
US retail sales fell 0.6% last month, marking the first drop in nine months and the biggest pullback since May 2025. 

We can blame June's compressed e-commerce spending for part of it. When Amazon, Walmart, and Target stacked their deal days early, nonstore retail spiked 7.7% in June before plunging 2.2% in July. 

🔍

Consumers didn't just take a breather—they hit a wall. Factor in an 8% drop in consumer sentiment and the unexpected loss of 23,000 jobs, and the picture gets tougher. 

The Fed has a delicate balance ahead of September. Are shoppers tapping out for good, or was July just an inevitable hangover?
